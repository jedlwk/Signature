# Voice samples

Real writing by Jed. Use these as anchors when a rule isn't enough. Rules describe the voice, but
samples show how it sounds, and research on style imitation says models do much better with real
examples than with descriptions.

## Contents
- How to use these

- Add a new sample

## How to use these

- Read one sample that matches the material before drafting. Match its rhythm and warmth, not its
  exact words.
- Don't copy phrases from a sample into new work. In testing, a smaller model copied "had the
  privilege of" straight into a new post. Borrowing it every time would become a tic of its own.
- The Medium post (2021) uses em dashes. Jed no longer wants them in anything Claude writes. Read
  the rhythm, skip the dashes.

## LinkedIn post

*Posted after the H2O.ai Executive HR Masterclass.*

> Where does AI actually belong in HR? How do you know if you need it, and how would you know you
> can trust it?
>
> Earlier this week, I had the privilege of spending the morning with over 30 HR leaders at our
> H2O.ai Executive HR Masterclass. Rather than just listen to me talk, I wanted the room to answer
> those questions themselves, with their laptop, without any coding. The energy in that room carried
> the whole morning!!!
>
> And that leads to the real question: if AI becomes a resource on your team, who manages it, and who
> checks its work? For me, that answer is in the heart of democratizing AI. The people who own the
> judgement should keep owning it.
>
> Being upskilled in AI means knowing where it fits in your own work and building on it.
> Non-technical teams can do that today, with planning, time and the right guidance. It won't happen
> in just one morning, but it adds up with every use case you try!
>
> My role has always been about closing the gap between what AI can do and what an organization is
> ready to trust, and this week that gap felt a little smaller.

**What to copy:** it opens on two real questions. One concrete morning with a real number. The
reader's work is the subject. One burst of energy. It ends on Jed's own role, quietly, with a
modest "a little smaller".

## Medium essay, selected lines

*From "[Personal] A Becoming 2021".*

> This is the first time I am penning down my year in writing. This will also be my first Medium
> Post. One of my 2022 Resolution is to consolidate my learning and become a better storyteller. I
> think it is pretty obvious I am trying to achieve both here.

> I am someone who likes to have strong control over my time and the things I do, with a clear
> understanding of what I want to achieve out of it. I like things planned, and I like things to go
> as planned. However, that is not always the case, especially so in a foreign place.

> What fascinates me about working in a Startup is that I get to narrate my own journey. I decide
> how much effort I am putting into a particular project or task. I decide the content and
> direction of my own experiments and creativity. I decide how much of an impact I aspire to make.

> Seems daunting? It sure was.

> I admit I am guilty of this in so many occurrences over the past 1 year, and before.

> Carpe Diem, and expect the unexpected.

**What to copy:** says plainly why he is writing. "I am" and "it is" more than contractions. States
what is true about himself directly. Uneven repetition that earns its place. Short closing lines on
their own. Admits fault in one line.

## Team message

> Six months ago this was just a couple of slides and big jargons... it's been a genuine pleasure
> building alongside all of you!!

**What to copy:** a past-to-present line, thanks that sounds meant, and one burst of exclamation
marks, because this is a message to teammates.

## Course writing

> My stance is yes.

> I think the biggest barrier to cross-socioeconomic connection isn't access, it's trust.

> Bad posture. Getting a bit shorter. Just aging.

> Giving out phones and going through clinics she already visits are the right calls. But phone
> numbers change, shift work makes schedules unpredictable, and knowing your risk window only helps
> if something in that window can change.

> Hi [name]!! great response! You said that it's a problem for everyone except the top 20 percent, but
> from the Wilkinson talk, it pushes that line even further than you did.

**What to copy:** the stance comes first. Real-life examples. Plain connectors (But, So). Fragments
in small doses. A casual greeting on a reply, then one specific thing.
