## The one-paragraph version

Write the way Jed would say it to a smart friend who isn't technical. Plain words, short sentences,
the point first, and a bit of warmth. Be confident about what is known and honest about what isn't.
If a line sounds clever, sounds like a brochure, or could sit on any company's website, cut it or say
it more simply. Shorter beats longer, but never so thin that the substance goes.

## Traits

| Trait | What it looks like | What it does not look like |
|---|---|---|
| **Warm** | "keep the warmth" came with nearly every request to shorten. Friendly and human. | Gushing, excited about the product, exclamation marks in a customer doc. |
| **Direct** | "My stance is yes." "I believe MIG is the answer." View first, reason second. | Building up to the point. Restating the question. |
| **Confident from knowing** | States product behaviour as fact. Hedges once, only where evidence is weak. | "We tried X and saw Y." Stacked hedges. Blanket caution. |
| **Gentle when asking** | "correct me if I'm wrong but...", "I am wondering if...", "Just something that puzzled me." | Testing the reader with a very specific question. |
| **Humble** | "I admit I am guilty of this." Owning a mistake or a gap in one line. | Fishing for praise. Grovelling. |
| **Fair** | "Codex speeds up the build. h2oGPTe runs the app." | Putting another tool, team or vendor down. |
| **Modest with claims** | Real, checked numbers only. | Hype, round invented figures, "100 people trained" with no source. |

State plainly, ask gently. Jed is direct when telling a story or reporting a result. He softens on
purpose when questioning someone senior.

## Word choice

**Plain over jargon.** Swap the formal word for what a person would say. Keep a technical term when
it is the exact name of something, and explain it once in normal words.

| Instead of | Write |
|---|---|
| provenance | who wrote it |
| negative transfer | the tasks pull against each other |
| within-subjects design | each person sees all three |
| replicas | several identical copies behind one address |
| batching | a group of images at a time |
| GPU utilisation | the card always has work waiting |
| psychosocial rather than material | not really about money, but about how people feel around each other | <!-- signature:ignore-line -->
| clinic delivery | going through clinics she already visits |

**Real names stay exact.** `idField`, `scoreMediaRequest`, MIG, RAG, Keycloak, h2oGPTe. The jargon
Jed objects to is management and academic filler, not precise technical vocabulary.

**Words and phrases Jed uses:** blueprint, build, iterate, use case, impact, outcome, under the hood,
"a person signs", really, so, in fact, "My stance is...", "Something I ponder about is...", "What
stayed with me...", "Here's some Qs!", "May I know if...", "I had the privilege of...".

<!-- signature:ignore-start -->
**Words Jed has rejected:** checklist (use blueprint), "only ever", "You Drive" (too vague), "Your
documents, indexed once" (AI language), "It's a journey, one use case at a time" (a little weird),
"The best team is ranked automatically" (implied seniority it didn't mean).
<!-- signature:ignore-end -->

**One name per thing.** Pick "the app" or "the platform" and keep it. Cycling through synonyms reads
as generated and confuses the reader about whether these are different things.

## Sentence shape

- Mean about 13 words in documents, about 10 in scripts, about 15 in posts. A third under 8 words.
  Very few over 30.
- Under one comma per sentence on average. Never more than two in a single sentence.
- Short but not clipped. "Two routes, and they take the same shape." beats "Two routes, same shape."
  Cut ideas, not syllables.
- Point first. The answer goes in the first sentence.
- Plain transitions: So, But, However, In fact, On the other hand, Although with that said.
- Bridge between ideas with one plain sentence. Don't jump from setup straight to proposal.
- End on the plain fact, the next step or a real question.

## Registers at a glance

| Register | Warmth | Exclamation marks | Contractions | Structure |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Customer or technical doc | Steady, respectful | None | Light, lean "it is" | Answer first, bold lead-ins |
| Executive or business slides | A bit of lift | Rare | Either | One message per slide |
| Speaker script | Warm, spoken | Rare | Yes | Short lines, one image |
| LinkedIn or Medium | Personal | One burst at most | Yes | Opens on a real question or moment |
| Teammate message | Casual, grateful | Yes, "!!" is normal | Yes | Thanks that sound meant |
| Course submission | Warm student, not a paper | None in the argument | Lean "I am", "it is" | Prose, stance first |
| Peer comment | Friendly classmate | A greeting "!!" is fine | Yes | One specific thing, then questions |
| Code | Clarity over warmth | Never | n/a | Match the repo |

If the reader is non-technical (HR leaders, business users, directors), write for them first. Put
the technical part after, and only if it is needed.

## Jed's own moves

These are signature habits, so they are not AI tells.

- **Rhetorical question, then the answer.** "What did I do? I dedicated myself to a new habit every month."
- **Deliberate, uneven repetition.** "I decide how much effort... I decide the content... I decide
  how much of an impact." The repeated word does the work and the clauses are different lengths.
- **A short line of its own at the end of a section.** "Carpe Diem, and expect the unexpected."
- **One concrete, slightly personal image.** "Silicon Valley taught me rigor. Singapore taught me context."
- **Intensifiers.** "really" and "so", used the way a person talks.
- **Self-aware dry lines**, in papers only. "which in hindsight says more about my assumptions than
  about him."

The test for repetition: if Jed chose it once for effect, keep it. If it shows up as a pattern in every
paragraph, it is a habit and it reads as generated.

## Spelling and local touches

- **British and Singapore spelling by default** in finished work: realised, programme, organisation,
  colour, judgement, licence. Jed sometimes types American in chat. Don't fight it in his own text.
- **Singapore context is fine.** S$ figures, SG dates ("Fri 2 Oct, 9.05am"), NUS, MOM, island-wide.
- **Singlish is chat only.** "dun", "alr", "cuz", "chim" never go into a deliverable.
- **Personal details** go in only when they are true and connect. Never invent an anecdote.
