# Posts and messages

The most personal register. LinkedIn and Medium posts, messages to teammates, emails, peer comments,
and short pieces like names and taglines. Checker registers: `post`, `message`.

## LinkedIn posts

Skeleton, taken from the HR Masterclass post:

1. **A real question** the reader already has.
2. **One concrete moment**, in first person. "Earlier this week, I had the privilege of spending the
   morning with over 30 HR leaders."
3. **The idea**, one idea carried through. Not a list of takeaways.
4. **A single burst of energy** is fine ("!!!"), once.
5. **A closing line about Jed's own view or role**, not a slogan.

Rules:

- "I" throughout. Warm and specific.
- Short paragraphs of one to three sentences.
- No hashtag stuffing, no emoji rows, no "Thrilled to announce". <!-- signature:ignore-line -->
- Credit people by name where the real post would.
- Use real numbers only ("over 30 HR leaders").

## Medium and personal essays

The Medium post, "A Becoming 2021", is the model.

- Open with a plain statement of why you are writing.
- Number the sections. Each one has a short title, an epigraph quote, the story, the lesson, and a
  short closing line of its own.
- Mix medium sentences with short ones. "It sure was."
- Rhetorical question, then the answer. "What did I do? I dedicated myself to a new habit every month."
- Admit fault in one line. "I admit I am guilty of this."
- Light humour is fine here and nowhere else. "( Don't judge me…)"
- Close each section on a line that stands alone.
- Dashes in the old post are Jed's. Don't copy them into new writing.

## Messages to teammates

- Casual and grateful. "Hi [name]!" and thanks that sound meant.
- "!!" is normal on chat. One exclamation mark at the end of an email is fine.
- Past to present in one line: "Six months ago this was a couple of slides and a lot of big words.
  Last Friday it was a working demo."
- Give the answer in the first line when someone asks a direct question. "Hi [name], I'll take
  these 6 then."
- Defer a little to a team lead without grovelling. "May I know if you are still looking for
  teammates?"

## Emails to clients

*Likely*, little direct evidence. Same voice as customer docs.

- Warm and steady. Answer first, then the detail, then what happens next.
- No exclamation marks and no open loops.
- Thanks, once, and meant.

## Peer comments and replies

For a course roundtable or discussion thread.

1. "Hi [name]!" and a thanks.
2. One specific thing that stuck. Not general praise.
3. "Here's some Qs!" with two or three simple, open questions, usually from Jed's own point of view.
4. A warm sign-off. "Really cool idea though. Good luck with it!"

For replies in a discussion forum, one paragraph. Pick the one thing in their post worth answering.
Agree, push back gently, or extend it with the reading. Then add Jed's angle.

## Asking questions

- Open and honest. Not so specific that it tests the reader, and not one Jed could answer by checking.
- Frame from Jed's side: "I was reading through... and I found that... I'm interested to know if..."
- Soften when asking someone senior: "correct me if I'm wrong but it seems to me that...", "Just
  something that puzzled me."

## Names, taglines and titles

- Give 3 to 5 real options and say which you would pick.
- Concrete, not generic. If an option is too specific, loosen it.
- One line each. No full stop at the end of a tagline.
- Avoid vague labels ("You Drive") and anything that implies something unintended ("ranked
  automatically" implied seniority).
